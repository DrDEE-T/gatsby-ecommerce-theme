# Pip & the Puddle Patrol
## Episode 1: "The Lost Ladybug"

Runtime: about 6 minutes
Audience: ages 1 to 5
Format: 2D animation, bright primary colors, soft rounded shapes
Learning goals: counting to 5, colors (red, yellow, green, blue), helping friends, "everyone helps in their own way"

---

## Cast & Voice Direction

| Character | Look | Voice | Signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pip | Tiny yellow duckling, red rain boots, blue cape made from a sock | High, squeaky, bouncy. Big gasps ("GASP!") before every idea. | "Quack-quack-QUACK! Puddle Patrol to the rescue!" |
| Gus | Big green turtle, mossy shell, sleepy smile | Deep, warm, grandpa-slow. Long pauses between words. | "No... need... to... rush." |
| Bitsy | Small lime-green frog, oversized goggles | Fast, breathless, words tumbling together, voice cracks up when excited. | "Ooh-ooh-ooh! Hop-to-it!" |
| Dot | Teeny red ladybug, 5 black spots | Tiny, soft, sing-song. Not scared, just lost. | "Hello? Mama?" |
| Mama Bug | Bigger ladybug | Gentle, motherly. | "My little Dot!" |
| Narrator | Off screen | Calm, friendly, slightly playful. Speaks to the kids directly. | "Can you help?" |

Casting note: Gus and Bitsy talking at the same time is the core gag. Record them separately so you can overlap them cleanly in the edit.

## Sound Library

| Code | Sound | Use |
|---|---|---|
| SFX-SPLASH | Big cartoon splash with a "bloop" tail | Every single jump. Never skip it. Kids will expect it. |
| SFX-HERO | "Quack-quack-QUACK!" with a rising brass sting | Hero call. Always the same pitch and timing. |
| SFX-SQUELCH | Rubber boots squishing in mud | Pip walking. Two-step rhythm: squelch-squish. |
| SFX-RAIN-LIGHT | Soft pitter-patter | Bed under Act 1 |
| SFX-RAIN-HEAVY | Steady rain, no thunder cracks | Flood scene. Use a soft distant "rumble" only, never a sharp crack. |
| SFX-ZOOM | Quick whoosh + tiny "zip" | Bitsy moving |
| SFX-CREAK | Slow, low wooden creak | Gus moving. Pairs with a slow tuba note. |
| SFX-BOING | Spring boing | Bitsy hops |
| SFX-DING | Bright xylophone ding | Each number counted, each correct answer |
| SFX-POP | Bubble pop | Transitions, Dot appearing |
| SFX-GASP | Pip's gasp + sparkle chime | Pip's idea moments |
| SFX-TADA | Short orchestral "ta-da!" | Rescue complete |
| SFX-BUZZ | Tiny wing flutter | Dot and Mama Bug flying |

Music
- MUS-THEME: Puddle Patrol theme. Ukulele, kazoo, hand claps. 120 BPM.
- MUS-SEARCH: Tiptoe pizzicato strings. Curious, never spooky.
- MUS-RESCUE: Theme melody sped up with marching drum.
- MUS-RAINBOW: Soft glockenspiel version of the theme.

Audio rule: keep the whole mix within a tight loudness range (aim for about -16 LUFS). No sudden spikes. Toddlers watch on phones held close to their faces.

## Transitions

| Code | Transition | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| TR-SPLASH | Water splash fills the screen, drips away to reveal new scene | Main scene change |
| TR-DROP | Giant raindrop falls toward camera, iris opens inside it | Into the rain |
| TR-BOOTS | Row of red boot prints stomps across screen as a wipe | Pip leads the way |
| TR-BUBBLE | Bubbles float up and pop the scene away | Soft, calm moments |
| TR-SPIN | Lily pad spins in, spins out | Bitsy's fast moments |
| TR-RAINBOW | Rainbow arc sweeps left to right | The happy ending |

Pacing rule: cut or move the camera every 3 to 5 seconds. Hold still only for "your turn" pauses, where kids answer.

### THEME SONG (0:15 to 0:45)

Simple, repeatable, sing-along. Lyrics on screen with a bouncing raindrop over each word.

> Splish! Splash! Who's that coming?
> Pip and Gus and Bitsy running!
> (GUS, spoken: "I'm... walking.")
> When a friend needs help today,
> Puddle Patrol is on the way!
> Quack-quack-QUACK!

Visual beats: Pip jumping puddle to puddle (SFX-SPLASH on every beat), Bitsy hopping in fast circles, Gus walking one step across the whole song and arriving on the final "QUACK!" exactly as it lands.

End on a title card: "Episode 1: The Lost Ladybug." Title card shaped like a ladybug, spots pop on one at a time (SFX-POP x5).

## Production Notes

Why this holds a toddler's attention
- Kids get a job about every 30 to 40 seconds: shout the catchphrase, count, spot Dot, chant.
- The hero call happens 4 times. By the third, kids yell it before Pip does. That's the goal.
- The slow vs. fast gag pays off: Gus's slowness becomes the solution, which gives the lesson without a lecture.
- The hidden Dot in every search shot rewards kids who watch closely and gives them a reason to rewatch.

Pause timing
- Every "your turn" moment needs a 2-second hold with the character looking straight at camera and leaning in. Don't fill it with music. Let a soft hum or the rain sit underneath.

Visual style
- Thick outlines, flat bright colors, big expressive eyes.
- Keep backgrounds simple so characters stand out.
- No flashing or strobing (photosensitivity safety).
- Water is always playful. Nobody is in danger, nobody cries.

Voice recording
- Record Pip's gasps as a separate pass so you have a library to drop in.
- Gus's lines: let the actor take real breaths between words. Don't time-stretch in post; it sounds robotic.
- Bitsy: record at natural speed, then you can nudge up 5 to 10% if needed. No more, or kids can't follow.
