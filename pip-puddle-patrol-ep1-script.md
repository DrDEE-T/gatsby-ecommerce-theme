# Pip & the Puddle Patrol
## Episode 1: "The Lost Ladybug"

Runtime: about 6.5 minutes
Audience: ages 1 to 5
Format: 2D animation, bright primary colors, soft rounded shapes
Learning goals: counting to 5, colors, being brave, helping friends, "every hero has a superpower"

Story spine: Pip is a hero in training with an empty badge spot on his cape. He wants a real hero badge. When a little ladybug gets stuck on a drifting leaf, each Patrol member uses their own hero power. Pip earns his badge by being brave, even though he's small and scared.

## Cast & Voice Direction

| Character | Look | Voice | Hero Power | Signature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pip | Tiny yellow duckling, red rain boots, blue sock cape with an empty badge spot | High, squeaky, bouncy. Big gasps ("GASP!") before every idea. | Bravery | "Quack-quack-QUACK! Puddle Patrol to the rescue!" |
| Gus | Big green turtle, mossy shell, sleepy smile | Deep, warm, grandpa-slow. Long pauses between words. | Strength | "No... need... to... rush." |
| Bitsy | Small lime-green frog, oversized goggles | Fast, breathless, words tumbling together. | Speed | "Ooh-ooh-ooh! Hop-to-it!" |
| Dot | Teeny red ladybug, 5 black spots | Tiny, soft, sing-song. A little worried, never panicked. | | "Help! Help, please!" |
| Mama Bug | Bigger ladybug with a little sash of badges | Gentle, warm, proud. | | "My little Dot!" |
| Narrator | Off screen | Calm, friendly, cheers along with the kids. | | "Can you help?" |

Casting note: Gus and Bitsy talking at the same time is the core comedy gag. Record them separately so you can overlap them cleanly in the edit.

Music
- MUS-THEME: Puddle Patrol theme. Ukulele, kazoo, hand claps. 120 BPM.
- MUS-HERO: Big brass and snare version of the theme. Plays for suit-up and the brave jump.
- MUS-SEARCH: Tiptoe pizzicato strings. Curious, never spooky.
- MUS-RESCUE: Theme melody with marching drum, building.
- MUS-BRAVE: Single soft piano, then swelling strings into MUS-HERO.
- MUS-RAINBOW: Glockenspiel version of the theme.

Audio rule: keep the whole mix within a tight loudness range (about -16 LUFS). No sudden spikes. Toddlers watch on phones held close to their faces.

## Production Notes

How the hero story works for toddlers
- Pip's want is set up in the first minute: an empty badge spot. Kids understand wanting something and getting it.
- Three heroes, three powers: fast, strong, brave. Each gets their own rescue moment, so the rescue has a clear 1, 2, 3 shape that small kids can follow.
- Each power fixes part of the problem but not all of it. That's the teamwork lesson, shown instead of told.
- Pip's bravery comes from the kids. They cheer him on, and he jumps. The viewer becomes part of the rescue.
- Kids get their own badge at the end. That's the moment they'll remember and want to rewatch.

Keeping it brave, not scary
- Dot is worried, never terrified. No crying, no screaming.
- The swirl is bright and bubbly, like a bathtub drain. Never dark water.
- Pip's scared moment is short (about 10 seconds) and the kids fix it right away.
- No flashing or strobing in the hero jump (photosensitivity safety). Use sparkles and slow motion instead.

Pause timing
- Every "your turn" moment needs a 2-second hold with the character looking straight at camera and leaning in. Don't fill the gap with music.
- The kids now have a job about every 25 to 30 seconds: hero call (x5), counting (x2), Hero Promise (x2), pointing, cheering, the swim chant.

The hero jump (4:03)
- This is the thumbnail shot and the clip to use for Shorts. Give it the most animation time in the budget.
- Hold slow motion for 3 to 4 seconds. Toddlers need time to take in a big moment.

Voice recording
- Record Pip's gasps as a separate pass so you have a library to drop in.
- Pip's "It's so far. And I'm so little" should be quiet and honest. The contrast makes the hero call land harder.
- Gus's lines: let the actor take real breaths between words. Don't time-stretch in post.
- Bitsy: record at natural speed, nudge up 5 to 10% max.
